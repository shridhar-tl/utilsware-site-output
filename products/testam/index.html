The difference

Most exam tools help you run one exam. Testam helps you run the next fifty.

The value isn't in delivering a test — it's in the bank of reviewed questions you keep afterwards, and in knowing which of them actually measure anything.

The usual way

  • Questions live in a document that nobody can search
  • Every new exam is rebuilt from scratch
  • Written answers pile up waiting for someone's evening
  • You learn who passed, but never which questions were doing the work
  • The good features are three pricing tiers away

With Testam

  • One question bank, categorised, reviewed, and reusable
  • Assemble an exam from material you already approved
  • Objective answers score themselves; AI drafts the rest
  • Difficulty and discrimination index on every question
  • Every capability, free, with no per-candidate fee

How it works

From a blank page to a published result

Five steps, and only the first one is writing. Everything after it is configuration you set once and reuse.

  1. 01

    Build the bank

    Write questions once — or generate a draft set with AI — and file them into a category tree. Markdown and media work in the question and in every option.

  2. 02

    Review and approve

    Questions move draft → pending review → approved. Moderators see exactly what's waiting on them, so nothing half-finished reaches a candidate.

  3. 03

    Assemble the exam

    Pick questions or whole sections, set marks and per-question time, choose navigation, shuffling, attempt limits, and how access is granted.

  4. 04

    Run it

    Candidates take it on any device with auto-save and a server-side timer. Proctoring signals, if you enabled them, are recorded as they happen.

  5. 05

    Grade and publish

    Objective answers are already scored. Subjective ones go to a grader or to AI. Then release scores, answers, and certificates on your schedule.

Grading

Three ways to grade, and you decide which answer gets which

Most of a paper grades itself. The part that genuinely needs judgement is the only part that reaches a person — and AI can draft even that.

Automatic

7 of 10 question types

Scored the moment the attempt is submitted — partial credit, negative marking, tolerance ranges, and position-aware ordering all included.

AI-assisted

Short answer · Long answer · Code

AI reads your rubric and proposes a score with its reasoning shown. It is a first pass, not a verdict — a grader can override any of it.

Manual

Anything you want to see yourself

A grading queue with the rubric beside the answer, inline comments, and the option to grade one question across every candidate at a time.

Scores, correct answers, and explanations are released on your schedule — immediately, after the deadline, after manual grading is finished, or never.

Proctoring

Off by default. On exactly as far as you need it.

Every measure is a per-exam switch, and candidates are shown which ones are active before they begin. Signals are written to a timestamped violation trail you review beside the attempt.

We won't pretend browser proctoring makes cheating impossible. It raises the cost and gives you evidence to look at — treat the log as an input to a human decision, not as a verdict.

  • Fullscreen enforcement

    Require fullscreen to start. On exit: warn, count it, auto-submit after N, or lock the attempt.

  • Focus & tab tracking

    Every time the window loses focus is timestamped, with a threshold you set.

  • Copy, paste & right-click

    Block the shortcuts outright, and log each blocked attempt.

  • Developer tools

    Detect when devtools are opened during an attempt.

  • Device restrictions

    Allow or refuse desktop, tablet, and mobile independently per exam.

  • Webcam & screen recordingExplicit opt-in

    Off unless you turn it on. Candidates are told before the attempt begins.

Live quiz

The same questions, played out loud

Turn any set of approved questions into a real-time room. It runs over Firestore, so the leaderboard moves the moment an answer lands rather than when someone refreshes.

  • Players join from any device with a short room code — no account, no install
  • You advance the questions; answers stream in and the board reorders as they land
  • Scoring rewards speed and streaks, so the room stays awake
  • Built on the same question bank as your exams — nothing to maintain twice

Analytics

Who passed is the least interesting thing an exam can tell you

The useful signal is which questions separated the people who knew the material from the people who didn't — and Testam measures that for you on every sitting.

  • Per examCompletion and abandonment, score distribution, time spent per question, and where attempts stall.
  • Per questionA difficulty index and a discrimination index — so you can retire the question everybody guesses right.
  • Per candidateTrends across attempts, strengths and weaknesses by category, and a history you can hand back to them.
  • Per organizationRoll-ups across teams, cohorts, and exams, so you can see the shape of a programme rather than a single sitting.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is Testam really free?›

Yes. Testam is free to use — for individuals, teams, schools, and companies alike. There is no trial period, no per-candidate charge, and no feature locked behind a paid tier. You can run unlimited exams, questions, and attempts.

Is Testam open source?›

No. Testam is free to use but its source code is proprietary and is not published. You use the hosted application; you don't self-host or modify it.

Do candidates need an account to take an exam?›

Not necessarily. It depends on how you configure access. An exam can be opened to anyone with the link, gated behind a passcode, restricted to a whitelist of email addresses, or protected with a one-time passcode delivered by email or SMS. Only exams that need to be tied to a member of your organization require the candidate to sign in.

What question types are supported?›

Ten: single-choice, multiple-choice, true/false, fill in the blank, short answer, long answer, matching, ordering, numerical, and code. Questions and every individual option support markdown and attached media, so diagrams, snippets, audio, and video all work.

How does automatic grading handle subjective answers?›

Objective types are graded the instant an attempt is submitted, including partial credit and negative marking where you've configured them. Short answer, long answer, and code go to a grading queue where you can grade manually, ask AI to grade against your rubric, or use AI as a first pass that a human then confirms or overrides. AI never has the last word unless you decide it does.

What does proctoring actually do?›

Proctoring is off by default and configured per exam. When enabled it can require fullscreen, detect and count exits, track window and tab focus loss, and block copy-paste, right-click, and developer tools. Webcam snapshots and screen recording are separate options you must turn on deliberately. Every signal is recorded as a timestamped violation you can review alongside the attempt — and candidates always see which measures are active before they begin.

Can proctoring stop someone from cheating entirely?›

No tool can, and we won't claim otherwise. Browser-based proctoring raises the cost of cheating and gives you an evidence trail to review; it is not a guarantee. For high-stakes certification, treat the violation log as one input into a human decision, not as a verdict.

How is the live quiz mode different from a normal exam?›

A live quiz is host-driven and synchronous. You open a room, players join with a short code from any device, and you advance the questions yourself while answers stream in and the leaderboard reorders in real time. It draws from the same question bank as your exams, so material you built for an assessment can be replayed as a warm-up or a revision session.

Can several people work on the question bank together?›

Yes. An organization has roles — admin, moderator, instructor, and member. Instructors create questions and exams, moderators review and approve them, and admins manage everything including users and settings. Questions move through draft, pending review, and approved states so a reviewer sees exactly what is waiting on them.

Where is our data stored, and who can see it?›

Exams, questions, attempts, and results are stored on our servers so they can be delivered and graded — this is a hosted platform, not a local tool. Within your organization, access is enforced by role: members see only their own attempts. We never sell your data, never use it for advertising, and never use it to train models.

What do the AI features send to a third party?›

Only when you invoke them, and only what the request needs. AI question generation sends the topic or prompt you supply. AI grading sends the question, the rubric, and the candidate's answer. Nothing is sent automatically, and account credentials are never included. If you don't use these features, no content leaves the platform.

Can I brand exams and certificates for my organization?›

Yes. Organizations can upload a logo and set branding that appears across exam pages and generated certificates. Certificate templates are customisable, and each issued certificate carries a unique verification code and QR that anyone can check without an account.

Does it work on phones and tablets?›

Yes — the whole platform is responsive, and candidates can take exams on a phone. You can also restrict an exam to specific device classes if, for example, a coding assessment only makes sense on a desktop.

Can I get my content out?›

Yes. Questions, exams, and results can be exported, and you can delete any content or your entire account at any time. Nothing you create is held hostage.

What happens if a candidate loses connection mid-exam?›

Answers auto-save as the candidate works, and the attempt keeps its own server-side timer. Reconnecting resumes the attempt where it left off, with the remaining time intact — a dropped connection doesn't hand back extra minutes, and it doesn't lose work.
